# End-to-End Workflow

## Purpose

Use this workflow when a manuscript requires more than local editing. Reconstruct the scientific argument from the evidence upward.

## Phase 1 — Evidence inventory

Before rewriting, identify:
- study question or problem;
- study units, samples, datasets, or experimental conditions;
- measurements, variables, comparisons, models, or analyses;
- key figures and tables;
- robust quantitative findings;
- uncertain or weak findings;
- descriptive versus inferential analyses;
- practical or theoretical outputs, if any.

Create a compact evidence map:

| Evidence block | What was measured/analyzed | Robust finding | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not begin wording edits if the manuscript-level argument is unclear.

## Phase 2 — Diagnose the existing manuscript

Identify:
- the research question the manuscript claims to address;
- the question actually answered by the evidence;
- whether sections follow analysis chronology rather than scientific logic;
- repeated findings across sections;
- methods presented as novelty;
- conclusions stronger than the analyses;
- important relationships that exist in the evidence but are not articulated.

Use `manuscript_diagnosis.md`.

## Phase 3 — Define the take-home statement

Write one sentence that a reader should remember after a week.

A strong take-home statement usually contains:
- the main phenomenon or finding;
- the key relationship, contrast, or explanation;
- the broader scientific or practical significance.

Do not force a universal sentence template. Test the sentence:
- Is it directly supported by the study?
- Is it more than a methods summary?
- Is it broader than one isolated result?
- Does it express the paper's most important scientific contribution?

## Phase 4 — Build the argument structure

Use the structure that best matches the study. Common forms include:
- phenomenon → contrast → explanation → implication;
- question → evidence → relationship → interpretation;
- structure → process → outcome;
- intervention → effect → mechanism → consequence;
- observation → model/test → synthesis → generalization;
- problem → evaluation → comparison → recommendation.

Do not impose one template on every manuscript.

## Phase 5 — Rebuild Results

Order subsections so that each answers one distinct question and prepares the next.

A generic sequence might include:
1. data/model quality or primary descriptive result;
2. main contrasts or patterns;
3. relationships among key variables or analyses;
4. robustness, heterogeneity, or secondary tests;
5. integrative analysis or synthesis.

Use only the parts that fit the study.

Each subsection should contain:
- one question;
- the minimum evidence needed to answer it;
- one clear takeaway;
- a transition to the next question when useful.

Use `results_restructuring.md`.

## Phase 6 — Create a synthesis layer only when justified

A synthesis layer is useful when several analyses remain difficult to interpret separately.

Possible synthesis devices include:
- conceptual framework;
- empirical grouping;
- comparison matrix;
- composite index;
- latent dimension;
- state/process diagram;
- decision framework;
- relationship map.

Before creating one, ask:
- Does it compress genuine information rather than re-label existing results?
- Is it reproducible?
- Does it answer a scientific question not already answered by simpler analyses?
- Is uncertainty acknowledged?

Do not invent a synthesis device merely to make the paper appear novel.

## Phase 7 — Reconstruct Discussion

For each major discussion unit, move through as many of the following as the evidence justifies:

> Result → Meaning → Explanation → Literature comparison → Implication → Limitation

Not every paragraph needs all six elements, but the reasoning should advance rather than repeat Results.

Use `discussion_reasoning.md`.

## Phase 8 — Reverse-engineer Introduction

After the argument is stable, ensure the Introduction contains:
1. why the problem matters;
2. what is already known;
3. the specific unresolved gap;
4. the conceptual or analytical bridge used to address it;
5. the study objective/questions in a clear progression.

Describe the study system only to the extent needed to justify why it is informative.

Delete method tutorials unless the method itself is central to the scientific gap.

Use `intro_conclusion_abstract.md`.

## Phase 9 — Rebuild Conclusion and Abstract

Conclusion:
- answer the study-level question;
- summarize only the most important findings;
- state the key relationship and its limits;
- end with the broader significance.

Abstract:
- problem/gap;
- approach;
- strongest findings only;
- main scientific contribution;
- implication.

## Phase 10 — Adapt to target journal

After the science is stable:
- adjust emphasis and terminology;
- change the balance between theory, application, method, or policy as appropriate;
- update title, abstract, highlights, and cover letter consistently.

Never alter the evidence to fit a journal.

## Recommended revision order

When the manuscript is structurally weak, prefer:

> Evidence → Results → Argument → Discussion → Introduction → Conclusion → Abstract → Title → Submission materials

This is often more reliable than editing from page 1 to the end.
