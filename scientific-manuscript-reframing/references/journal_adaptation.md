# Journal Adaptation

## Purpose

Adapt a scientifically stable manuscript to a target journal without changing the underlying evidence.

## Step 1 — Read the journal's actual scope

Identify what the journal prioritizes, for example:
- theory or mechanism;
- empirical generality;
- methodological innovation;
- environmental or societal relevance;
- policy or management application;
- engineering performance;
- clinical relevance;
- interdisciplinary integration.

Do not rely only on the journal title or impact factor.

## Step 2 — Inspect recent comparable papers

When the user requests journal-fit research, examine recent papers for:
- question framing;
- article length and structure;
- level of methodological detail;
- strength of generalization;
- balance of theory and application;
- style of titles, abstracts, and highlights.

Use these observations to adjust emphasis, not to imitate wording.

## Step 3 — Match the manuscript's strongest contribution

Choose the framing that is both true and relevant to the journal.
Examples:
- emphasize a general relationship for a theory-oriented journal;
- emphasize validation and reproducibility for a methods journal;
- emphasize decision consequences for an applied journal;
- emphasize cross-domain integration for an interdisciplinary journal.

## Step 4 — Rebalance sections

Possible changes include:
- shorten background that the target audience already knows;
- expand the theoretical or practical interpretation the journal values;
- move technical detail to supplementary material when allowed;
- reduce local context when broader significance is required;
- retain context when local application is central to the journal.

## Step 5 — Align submission materials

### Title
State the phenomenon or contribution before the method when possible.

### Abstract
Make the research gap and contribution visible early.

### Highlights
Prefer findings or advances over procedural statements.

### Cover letter
Explain:
1. what problem the paper addresses;
2. what is new;
3. why it matters;
4. why the journal's readers are the right audience.

Do not flatter the journal or claim novelty that the manuscript cannot support.

## Cost and format checks

When relevant, verify:
- word/page limits;
- open-access requirements;
- APC or page charges;
- article type;
- figure/table limits;
- supplementary-material policy.

Use current official sources when these details can change.
